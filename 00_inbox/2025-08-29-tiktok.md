# TikTok Video Quality Tips

## Camera Settings (iPhone 15)
- **Resolution**: Record at 4K, 30 fps  
  - 4K gives TikTok more data to compress down  
  - 30 fps aligns with TikTok’s processing rate  
  - For fast-moving content (e.g., sports, dance), use 60fps (though compression may introduce artifacts)  
- **HDR Video**: Turn OFF  
  - TikTok does not process HDR well; colors can appear washed out after upload  
- **Format**: Use High Efficiency (HEVC) instead of “Most Compatible”  
  - Saves space; TikTok handles HEVC well  
- **Stabilization**: Keep ON (especially for handheld shots)

### iPhone Settings
- Go to **Settings > Camera > Record Video**  
- Set to **4K at 30fps**  
- Toggle **HDR Video OFF**

## Shooting Tips
- **Lighting**: Lighting is king  
  - Low-light footage is heavily damaged by compression  
  - Bright, even lighting produces sharper videos  
- **Backgrounds**: Plain or high-contrast scenes survive compression better than cluttered or dark scenes  
- **Avoid Zooming**: Digital zoom reduces sharpness, and compression worsens the result  

## Upload Workflow
- Edit externally (Final Cut, CapCut, DaVinci, or iMovie)  
  - Export at 4K, 30fps, high bitrate  
- Upload directly from TikTok app (not third-party apps or AirDrop-compressed versions)  
- Use **Wi-Fi upload** over a strong connection  
  - Poor connections cause extra compression  
- Turn **Data Saver OFF** in TikTok app (**Settings > Data Saver > OFF**)  

## Bonus
- **Cover Art/Thumbnails**:  
  - TikTok compresses preview frames more than the video itself  
  - Don’t stress if thumbnails look softer  

### CapCut Export Settings for TikTok (if editing in CapCut)
- Resolution: 4K  
- Frame rate: 30fps  
- Bitrate: 20–30 Mbps

## Resolution and Frame Rate
- Shoot in 4K (if possible) for maximum clarity and flexibility  
  - 1080p can still look good but 4K offers better detail  
- Use 30fps or 60fps depending on video feel  
  - Higher frame rates provide smoother motion, especially in action shots  

## Bitrate and File Format
- Use a high bitrate when exporting to preserve details  
- **MP4 with H.264 codec** is ideal for TikTok  
  - Offers the best balance of quality and file size  

## Lighting and Exposure
- Good lighting reduces noise and compression artifacts  
- Shoot in well-lit environments or use softboxes/LED lights  
- Avoid underexposure or overexposure, which increase visibility of compression artifacts  

## Editing and Compression
- Avoid excessive compression during editing  
- Export at 1080p (or 4K) with a high bitrate  
- Use optimized export settings in third-party editors like CapCut or Adobe Premiere  

## TikTok Upload Settings
- Upload in **portrait mode (9:16 aspect ratio)** to maximize screen real estate  
- Avoid using TikTok’s mobile app editor if possible  
  - It may compress your video further  
- Use native file upload instead

## Audio Compression on TikTok

TikTok re-encodes audio, so the goal is to provide clean, loud, and balanced input without clipping (to avoid distortion during recompression).

### Export from Your DAW
- **Peak level**: Target -6 dB (keeps headroom for encoding)  
- **Loudness (LUFS)**: Aim for -14 LUFS integrated (streaming standard)  
  - Prevents TikTok from squashing the audio too hard  
- **File format**: Export as WAV (44.1 kHz, 24-bit)  
  - Can be converted later; TikTok processes MP3/ACC more aggressively  

### Mastering (Manual)
- **EQ**: Cut low-mid mud (200–400 Hz); brighten highs if needed  
- **Compression**: Gentle glue (max 2–3 dB gain reduction)  
- **Limiter**: Ceiling at -1 dB True Peak (to avoid inter-sample clipping after recompression)  
- **Target loudness**: -14 LUFS integrated / -9 LUFS short-term (for music/beat drops)  

### Importing to CapCut
- Never reduce volume in CapCut unless clipping  
  - Setting to 88% means your master is too hot  
- Master to correct loudness upfront (-14 LUFS, -1 dB ceiling)  
- Leave CapCut at **100% volume**  
- CapCut exports in AAC (compressed) — TikTok recompresses again  
  - Starting clean and avoiding volume reduction preserves clarity  

### Final Export (CapCut → TikTok)
- Export video with AAC audio, 320 kbps, 44.1 kHz (if CapCut allows)  
- Do **not normalize or adjust gain** on export — keep consistency  

### TL;DR Workflow
1. DAW → export WAV: peak -6 dB, loudness ~-14 LUFS  
2. Master with limiter ceiling at -1 dB  
3. Import into CapCut at 100% volume  
4. Export at AAC 320 kbps  
5. Upload to TikTok

## Free Workflow in FL Studio

### Step 1: Set Up Master Bus in FL
- Open Master channel in Mixer  
- Add effects in this order:  
  1. Parametric EQ (clean low mud)  
  2. Compressor (FL Limiter in COMP mode — light glue)  
  3. Limiter (FL Limiter in LIMIT mode — final ceiling)  
  4. Loudness Meter plugin (free, see below)  

### Step 2: Free LUFS Meter
- FL Studio doesn’t include a built-in LUFS meter  
- Use **Youlean Loudness Meter 2 (FREE)** — industry standard  
- [youlean.co/youlean-loudness-meter](https://youlean.co/youlean-loudness-meter/)  

### Step 3: Dial in Levels
- Mix normally to achieve a balanced mix  
- On Master:  
  - EQ: High-pass at ~30 Hz; small cuts for mud  
  - Compressor: Ratio 2:1, attack ~20ms, release ~100ms (shaving 1–2 dB)  
  - Limiter: Ceiling at -1 dB  
  - Adjust gain until track reads ~-14 LUFS Integrated in Youlean  
- Check short-term LUFS on loud parts (drops/choruses)  
  - Target ~-9 LUFS for punchy but safe levels  

### Step 4: Export
- **File type**: WAV  
- **Sample rate**: 44.1 kHz  
- **Bit depth**: 24-bit  
- Leave **“Normalize” OFF**  
  - Keeps audio clean for CapCut/TikTok compression  

### Your Free FL Studio Workflow
1. Mix  
2. Master chain: EQ → Compressor → Limiter (ceiling -1 dB) → Youlean Loudness Meter  
3. Export WAV 44.1kHz / 24-bit  
4. Import into CapCut at 100% volume  
5. Export with best settings  
6. Upload to TikTok
