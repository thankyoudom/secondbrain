Absolutely. I’d turn the Mœbius/Toppi idea into a **long-horizon volume framework** inside your practice, rather than making “10,000 drawings” the goal. The useful takeaway is that their output came from decades of sustained practice, and your system should create a similar compounding effect.

# The High-Fidelity Practice

## Direction

Develop the ability to **see, make, explain, and connect** — with the long-term goal of becoming someone capable of leading a small technical/creative team.

The objective is not to become a generalist who dabbles in everything.

> **Build deep craft first. Let range grow around it.**

---

# Daily

## 1. 20 min — Visual Study

Study one image.

Ask:

* Why does this work?
* Where does my eye go first?
* How is the composition structured?
* What is the artist simplifying?
* How are value, color, light, and space being controlled?
* What makes the image recognizable as *this artist*?

Rotate between:

* illustration
* painting
* photography
* film
* architecture
* graphic design
* comics
* animation
* product/UI design

### Principle

**Don't just consume images. Learn to see them.**

---

## 2. 30–60 min — Drawing

Drawing is the **deepest creative practice**.

Prioritize:

* anatomy
* gesture
* perspective
* form
* light
* composition
* environment
* visual storytelling

The goal isn't simply to produce attractive drawings.

The goal is to develop the ability to **construct what you see in your head.**

### The Long Game

Think in **thousands of drawings**, not individual drawings.

Artists such as **Mœbius** and **Sergio Toppi** left enormous bodies of work because drawing was not an occasional creative activity. It was a lifelong language.

Don't worry about matching their lifetime output.

Instead:

> **Draw every day long enough for volume to become inevitable.**

### Approximate volume targets

| Period   | Possible output |
| -------- | --------------: |
| 1 month  |  15–30 drawings |
| 6 months |         100–200 |
| 1 year   |         200–400 |
| 3 years  |       600–1,200 |
| 5 years  |    1,000–2,000+ |
| 10 years |    2,000–4,000+ |

These numbers aren't quotas.

A 5-minute gesture study and a finished illustration both count as drawing. The important thing is **continued contact with the medium**.

Over years, sketches, studies, experiments, finished pieces, failures, and breakthroughs accumulate into a body of work.

---

## 3. 1 Paragraph — Writing

Observe something → articulate it → draw a conclusion.

Write about:

* something you noticed
* something you made
* something that bothered you
* something beautiful
* an engineering problem
* a design decision
* an interaction
* an idea from a book
* something you don't understand yet

### Principle

**Clear thinking requires articulation.**

Writing turns intuition into something you can inspect.

---

## 4. 1 High-Fidelity Observation

Notice something that could be better.

Ask:

> **Why is this wrong?**

Then:

> **How would I fix it?**

This can be anything:

* a website
* a restaurant menu
* an app interaction
* a camera interface
* a piece of music
* a drawing
* a product
* a room
* a workflow
* an engineering system
* a video

Train yourself to notice **friction, inconsistency, beauty, and missed opportunity.**

This is the beginning of creative direction.

---

## 5. Make Something

Something should exist at the end of the session that didn't exist before.

Possible outputs:

* sketch
* illustration
* beat
* photograph
* edit
* animation
* UI
* code
* visualization
* sound experiment
* prototype
* technical diagram

The output does not need to be impressive.

> **The practice is to repeatedly move from nothing → something.**

---

## 6. 1 Meaningful Creative Interaction

Talk to someone who makes things.

Possible people:

* artist
* engineer
* musician
* designer
* filmmaker
* athlete
* researcher
* founder
* architect
* creative director

Practice **curiosity rather than networking**.

Ask:

* What are you working on?
* What are you trying to get better at?
* What are you noticing lately?
* What do you think most people misunderstand about your craft?

The objective is to build a richer mental ecosystem.

---

# Weekly

## Director's Review — 30–45 min

Review everything made that week.

Don't judge yourself primarily on productivity.

Look for **patterns**.

Ask:

### Taste

* What keeps attracting me?
* What am I repeatedly drawn toward?
* What visual or technical qualities do I admire?

### Craft

* What is actually getting better?
* What still looks amateur?
* Where am I avoiding difficulty?

### Output

* What did I actually make?
* What remained unfinished?
* Am I producing enough volume to learn?

### Direction

* What keeps appearing without me forcing it?
* What should I stop doing?
* What deserves another month of exploration?

### Selection

Choose:

**1 thing to keep.**

**1 thing to improve.**

**1 thing to abandon.**

**1 thing to explore next week.**

---

# Monthly

## Body-of-Work Review

Once a month, look at the work **without remembering when you made it.**

Ask:

* Is there a recognizable taste emerging?
* Are certain subjects recurring?
* Is my composition improving?
* Is my drawing becoming more economical?
* Are my technical projects becoming more coherent?
* What would I make if nobody were watching?
* What would I make if I had to make only one thing for the next year?

Create a folder:

`/best/`

Only put work there that you would still respect six months later.

The purpose isn't to create a portfolio immediately.

It is to learn **selection**.

# The Compounding Model

Don't think:

> "I need to become good at drawing."

Think:

> **I need to become the person who has drawn for ten years.**

Don't think:

> "I need to make a great project."

Think:

> **I need to repeatedly make, review, and improve things.**

Don't think:

> "I need to find my style."

Think:

> **I need to make enough work for my style to reveal itself.**

Volume creates experience.

Experience creates judgment.

Judgment creates taste.

Taste determines what deserves refinement.

Refinement creates craft.

Craft creates a body of work.

---

# Don't Keep Adding Interests

Existing interests should **compound**, not compete.

Drawing improves:

→ observation
→ composition
→ visual communication
→ design judgment

Writing improves:

→ articulation
→ reasoning
→ communication
→ leadership

Engineering improves:

→ systems thinking
→ execution
→ technical fluency
→ ability to build

Music improves:

→ rhythm
→ listening
→ structure
→ emotional judgment

Physical practice improves:

→ coordination
→ stamina
→ discipline
→ presence

The goal is not five separate identities.

They should eventually become **one person with a coherent way of seeing and making.**

---

# Core Principle

> **See. Make. Explain. Connect.**

High standards, low pretension.

Make fewer things.

Make them better.

Develop taste.

Understand the medium.

Learn the technology.

Study people who are better than you.

Collaborate with people who are better than you.

Keep making.

---

# The Long-Term Goal

The goal isn't to become a generalist.

> **Become someone with deep craft, strong taste, technical fluency, and enough range to direct the whole thing.**

A person who can:

**See** what is possible.

**Make** enough to understand the craft.

**Explain** the idea clearly.

**Connect** the right people and disciplines.

And eventually:

> **Direct the whole system without needing to personally do every part of it.**

## That is the High-Fidelity Practice.
