# Ian Connor

## Early Life & Beginnings

Ian Connor is from New Jersey, U.S., and rose to visibility as a fashion-influenced youth in the early 2010s. (Dazed Digital)

In his teens, he ran a Tumblr blog, curating style images that blended streetwear, punk/grunge influences, and vintage finds. This helped build his aesthetic and network. (Brdlee)

He cultivated a persona: self-proclaimed “King of the Youth.” (Complex)

## Styling, Modeling, and Network Building

Connor initially gained exposure through styling work. For example, he began styling Wiz Khalifa while still young. (The Business of Fashion)

He also modeled for brands like BAPE and Stüssy, appearing in lookbooks and campaigns. (Complex)

His look and style caught the attention of major figures. He had close ties with A$AP Rocky and the A$AP Mob collective, and later with Kanye West’s fashion circle. (The Business of Fashion)

In 2016, *Vogue* described him as a “muse” of Kanye West’s fashion team. (The Business of Fashion)

## First Steps into Designing / Brand Launches

Around 2016, Connor launched his own sneaker concept under the label *Revenge X Storm* (“Revenge x Storm”). This marked a major pivot from styling/modeling into product design. (The Business of Fashion)

The *Revenge x Storm* sneaker was noted for its “Vans-inspired” low silhouette with a lightning bolt slash motif. (The Business of Fashion)

He later launched another streetwear label under the name *Sickö* (or “Sicko”), which became controversial in multiple ways. (The Fashion Law)

## Design Aesthetic & Cultural Impact

Connor’s design viewpoint is deeply rooted in combining vintage/underground streetwear (especially early 2000s hip-hop & skater style), punk influences, thrift culture, and internet/meme aesthetics. (Silver Screen Magazine)

His influence extends beyond his own brand. By styling key youth culture figures and blending subcultures, he helped shape the aesthetic of a generation of streetwear. (Many credit him for pushing the “tumblr youth fashion” mainstream streetwear wave.) (Brdlee)

However, his design output has been heavily critiqued. Some in the community argue his designs contain heavy appropriation—borrowing or adapting visuals from other artists or brands. (Reddit)

## Major Collaborations & Industry Recognition

Due to his network, Connor collaborated or worked indirectly with major brands and labels. His styling/consulting work connected him with the Yeezy line, among others. (citiMuzik)

He appeared in *Dazed’s* “Dazed 100” list, which singled him out as a key creative force of his generation. (Dazed Digital)

## Controversies & Decline in Standing

Starting around 2016, multiple allegations of sexual assault and rape were brought against Connor. Though formally charged in many cases, these allegations remain unresolved. (The Business of Fashion)

The design/brand side also faced legal trouble: the *Sickö* brand was sued by another brand that held the “SICKO” trademark, alleging Connor’s version infringed on the mark. (The Fashion Law)

His reputation in fashion media shifted. Many outlets questioned how his influence could persist given the allegations, and there was increasing scrutiny of his business practices. (The Business of Fashion)

## Current Status & Legacy

Today, Ian Connor remains a polarizing figure in streetwear. His early work and network had a significant impact on youth fashion in the 2010s—though this is tempered by controversies and critiques regarding design originality and ethics.

His design legacy is ambiguous: on one hand, he helped bring streetwear culture into more mainstream and high-fashion contexts; on the other, his own brand output is debated in terms of originality and longevity.

If you look at youth fashion trends—oversized fits, skater/hip-hop mashups, branding that feels “underground but hype”—you can trace some of that back to the movement he was part of.
