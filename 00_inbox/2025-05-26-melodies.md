# Melodies

If your melodies sound dry and lifeless, it’s likely due to insufficient harmonic content, narrow stereo width, or lack of movement. Let’s address this step by step with a complete, free-tool-based plan—including frequency ranges, panning strategies, plugin chains, and melody composition tips.

## Step 1: Melody Composition Techniques (Fix the Source First)

### A. Add Harmonic Movement
- Use chord tones and passing tones: Write melodies that move within the scale but resolve to chord tones from the progression.
- Layer in 3rds or 6ths: Harmonize your melodies by adding a second line moving in parallel 3rds or 6ths above or below.
- Implement counter-melodies: Add a second melodic voice that differs rhythmically or melodically but remains in key.

## Step 2: Frequency Range for Lushness

Target these frequency bands to achieve a fuller harmonic texture:
- Use multiple layers to contribute across different frequency ranges.
- Apply EQ to shape the overall tonal balance.

## Step 3: Free Plugin Effects Chain for Lush Melodies

Build this chain in FL Studio using only free plugins:

1. **Saturation / Harmonics**  
   - Adds warmth and subtle distortion, generating more harmonics.  
   - *Free Plugin Options*:  
     - Softube Saturation Knob or IVGI by Klanghelm  
     - FL’s built-in Fruity Blood Overdrive (with low gain)  
   - *Setup*:  
     - Apply subtly to mono instruments (e.g., sine waves) to thicken them.  
     - Use in parallel if the effect becomes too harsh.

2. **Chorus + Flanger (Better Alternatives)**  
   - You mentioned FL Chorus and Flangus are insufficient. Try:  
     - *Magic Switch* (by Baby Audio – free chorus)  
     - *Blue Cat’s Chorus* or *TAL-Chorus-LX* (Juno-style chorus)  
   - Stack two instances with different depths and rates for richer, more complex stereo movement.

3. **Delay (Adds Movement and Harmonics)**  
   - *Free Plugin*: Valhalla Supermassive (best free delay/reverb combo)  
   - Use presets like “Gemini” or “Hydra” for spacious, movement-rich effects.  
   - Apply to melodic stabs or leads using 1/8 or dotted 1/4 note intervals.  
   - Add light feedback to enhance depth.

4. **Reverb (Adds Space and Glue)**  
   - *Free Plugin Options*: Dragonfly Reverb or OrilRiver  
   - Apply a high-pass filter before reverb to remove low-end mud (<250 Hz).  
   - Set pre-delay to 20–4.0ms to separate dry and wet signals.

5. **EQ (Tone Sculpting)**  
   - *Free Plugin*: TDR Nova  
   - Boost gently at 6k–8k Hz to enhance brightness.  
   - Cut harsh mids at 800Hz–1.5kHz if needed.  
   - Add a low-shelf boost at 200Hz if the melody sounds too thin.

## Step 4: Panning and Stereo Tips

- **Main melody layer**: Keep centered or slightly off-center.  
- **Harmonic layer (3rd/6th above)**: Pan 20–40% left or right.  
- **Counter-melody**: Hard-pan opposite to your main secondary harmony.  
- **Reverb/delay returns**: Use a stereo imager (e.g., Ozone Imager 2 – free) to widen the stereo image.  
- *Pro Tip*: Automate pan or stereo width to introduce dynamic movement over time.

## Step 5: Layering for Harmonic Richness

Use at least three layers for harmonic depth:

- **Fundamental**: Mono lead with simple waveforms (sine, square).  
- **Mid Harmonic Layer**: Detuned saw or pulse waves, panned to L/R.  
- **Air/Sparkle Layer**: High-passed pad or noise layer, stereo-widened.  

### Example Plugin: Vital (Free Synth)  
- **Layer 1**: Sine wave + subtle distortion  
- **Layer 2**: Two detuned saw waves, panned left and right  
- **Layer 3**: White noise or high-passed saw, EQ’d to 6k–10k Hz only  

## Final Tips

- **Modulation = Life**: Automate cutoff filters, LFOs on pitch, or pan to create dynamic movement.  
- Use scale helpers in FL: Tools > Scale Highlighting, select your key.  
- Avoid overloading low mids (200–400Hz)—too much can cause muddiness.  
- Render harmonized melodies to audio and use reverse or stretched slices to create texture.
