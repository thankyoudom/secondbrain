# Writing at Yale Senior Societies

## Skull and Bones: Essay Writing and Curriculum

Skull and Bones is a secretive senior society at Yale University, with no publicly available curriculum or formal assignments such as essay writing (Wikipedia). However, campus folklore and student discussions suggest alternative practices:

### On GreekRank, a student-commenter claims:
> “Skull and Bones writes essays because obviously… they have these pretentious traditions. … Desmos is pretty serious and they have every write essays in response to the weekly bios.”

### Another adds:
> “They… focused on extremely long bios, essay writing, group discussions, etc.” (Greekrank)

### That said, others caution:
> “Not just Desmos literally every society… ask you to write a response to the person who just gave a bio — could be a paragraph or a letter.” (Greekrank)

**Bottom line**: There is no formal, confirmed curriculum for Skull and Bones. However, anecdotal reports suggest that many members—within Skull and Bones and similar Yale societies—engage in reflective writing, graduating class bios (essentially extended personal statements), group discussions, and possibly weekly written responses. This isn’t official, but it’s part of how members process and share personal narratives.

## Equivalent Groups and Their Practices

At Yale, the so-called “Big Three” senior societies are:
- Skull and Bones
- Scroll and Key
- Wolf’s Head

These groups are steeped in tradition and select members in their junior year. Their internal practices are closely guarded, but campus lore suggests similarly intense social and intellectual rituals—such as speeches, long bios, or writing—but there is no public curriculum available (Wikipedia).

Other traditional or debating societies (sometimes referred to as “Desmos” in student slang) apparently require essay-like autobiographies or thoughtful written responses. Discussions and eloquent narratives seem to be a core feature across multiple societies—even if it’s informal (Greekrank).

## Building a Daily Writing Habit

Inspired by these traditions, here’s a lightweight routine you can try:

### Daily micro-writing (10–20 min):
- Reflect on your day
- Jot thoughts
- Write brief responses to prompts like:  
  - “What challenged me today?”  
  - “One idea I want to explore further.”

### Weekly deeper piece (30–6 & 60 min):
- Similar to the “bio” tradition—craft a well-thought-out essay or personal narrative  
- It could be about a topic you care about, a personal reflection, or a creative piece.

### Sharing or reflecting:
- Even if not in a secret society, sharing your work with a friend or journal can create accountability and added meaning.

By weaving shorter daily writing with a weekly richer piece, you’ll build consistency and depth—just like how traditions in these societies seem to encourage reflection over time.

## Prince Mohammed bin Salman and the “Book a Week + Essay” Practice

You’d heard that Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman (MBS) used to read a book a week and write an essay. I couldn’t find any credible confirmation that he followed this specific habit. There are, however, references to his interest in Machiavelli, plus classics like Churchill and Sun Tzu’s *The Art of War* (Religion Unplugged, Siasat.pk Forums). But the “book a week + essay” idea appears to be unverified—possibly a myth.
