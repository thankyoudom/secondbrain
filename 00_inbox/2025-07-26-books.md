# Books: Recommendations from Peter Thiel, Sam Altman, Jensen Huang, and Jack Dorsey

## Peter Thiel’s Favorite & Recommended Books

Thiel has frequently cited both fiction and non-fiction that shape his worldview:

- **The Lord of the Rings** — His favorite in youth; admired for its heroic journey and exploration of power dynamics, alliances, and moral corruption ([kevinrooke.com](https://kevinrooke.com)).
- **100 Plus by Sonia Arrison** — Praised in 2012 for its vision of life-extension and anti-aging science ([Farnam Street](https://farnamstreet.com)).
- **New Atlantis by Francis Bacon** — A classic vision of state-sponsored science and utopian technocracy ([Farnam Street](https://farnamstreet.com)).
- **The American Challenge by Jean-Jacques Servan-Schreiber** — On the informational economy and the changing nature of power ([Farnam Street](https://farnamstreet.com)).
- **The Great Illusion by Norman Angell** — Offers a historical critique of military power vs national advantage (Note: Angell is the author, not Bacon) ([Farnam Street](https://figuous.com)).
- **The Diamond Age by Neal Stephenson** — A science-fiction novel engaging with nanotech and AI possibilities ([Farnam Street](https://farnamstreet.com)).

### Additional Favorites (from broader lists)

- *The Sovereign Individual* by James Dale Davidson & William Rees-Mogg  
- *Psychopolitics & Things Hidden…* by René Girard  
- *Originals* by Adam Grant  
- *7 Powers* by Hamilton Helmer  
- *Capital in the Twenty-First Century* by Thomas Piketty  
- *The Decadent Society* by Ross Douthat  
- *Life After Google* by George Gilder  
- *The Hard Thing About Hard Things* by Ben Horowitz (Venture Book Club)

## Sam Altman’s Recommended Reading

Altman often emphasizes books that span philosophy, AI, psychology, and entrepreneurship:

### Core Titles He’s Frequently Named

- **The Beginning of Infinity** by David Deutsch — “One of the most important books I’ve ever read,” on knowledge, progress, and boundless learning ([LinkedIn](https://linkedin.com)).
- **Zero to One** by Peter Thiel — Startup strategy insight on creating new value instead of competitive markets ([theinfluentialbooks.com](https://theinfluentialbooks.com)).
- **Thinking, Fast and Slow** by Daniel Kahneman — Deep dive into cognitive biases and decision-making processes ([theinfluentialbooks.com](https://theinfluentialbooks.com)).
- **Man’s Search for Meaning** by Viktor Frankl — Lessons on purpose, resilience, and perspective under pressure ([theinfluentialbooks.com](https://theinfluentialbooks.com), [elmure.com](https://elmure.com)).
- **Blitzscaling** by Reid Hoffman & Chris Yeh — On how to scale startups rapidly, even at the risk of turbulence ([theinfluentialbooks.com](https://theinfluentialbooks.com)).
- **Superintelligence** by Nick Bostrom — A serious exploration of AI risk and strategic alignment ([LinkedIn](https://linkedin.com)).
- **The Hard Thing About Hard Things** by Ben Horowitz — A practical guide to managing adversity in building and leading tech companies ([LinkedIn](https://linkedin.com)).
- **The Lean Startup** by Eric Ries — Validated learning and efficient experimentation in early product development ([LinkedIn](https://linkedin.com)).
- **Secrets of Sand Hill Road** by Scott Kupor — Inside view of venture capital and deal-making dynamics ([LinkedIn](https://linkedin.com)).
- **Winning** by Jack Welch — Leadership lessons from GE’s CEO on building organizational excellence ([LinkedIn](https://linkedin.com)).
- **Meditations** by Marcus Aurelius — Stoic philosophy on leadership, self-discipline, and clarity in chaos ([Hawkdive.com](https://hawkdive.com)).
- **Range** by David Epstein — Emphasizes the value of breadth over narrow specialization in complex environments ([Hawkdive.com](https://hawkdive.com)).
- **Deep Work** by Cal Newport — Advocates for focused work amidst distraction to produce high-value outcomes ([Hawkdive.com](https://hawkdive.com)).

### Other Books Mentioned

- *Brave New World* (Huxley)  
- *High Output Management* (Andy Grove)  
- *The Republic* (Plato)  
- *Dealers of Lightning*  
- Various fiction/classics like *Anna Karenina* ([elmure.com](https://elmure.com))

## Jensen Huang (Nvidia) – Book Influence & Biographies

While Huang hasn’t publicly listed reading recommendations, two recent biographies shed light on his leadership and tech philosophy:

- **The Thinking Machine** by Stephen Witt  
- **The Nvidia Way** by Tae Kim  

These books describe his strategic pivot from graphics to AI, the creation of CUDA, and his intense, hands-on leadership style. They also note his avoidance of speculative futurism in favor of immediate technical progress ([The Guardian](https://theguardian.com), [Financial Times](https://ft.com)).

## Suggestions for Next Steps

- **For Thiel**: Explore *The Sovereign Individual* and *Things Hidden…* if interested in the ideological basis of his worldview ([Financial Times](https://ft.com), [Reddit](https://reddit.com), [The Guardian](https://theguardian.com)).
- **For Altman**: Start with *Thinking, Fast and Slow*, *The Beginning of Infinity*, and *Man’s Search for Meaning* — these offer cognitive frameworks, philosophical grounding, and insights into resilience.
- **For Huang**: The two biographies are the primary lens into his thinking and leadership philosophy — they aren’t at the recommendation level, but are hugely informative if you're curious about how he built Nvidia.

## Jack Dorsey’s Top Books

Jack Dorsey has shared his favorite and most impactful books over the years, often highlighting them in interviews and via X (Twitter). Several sources list recurring titles, giving insight into his values and interests ([Business Insider](https://businessinsider.com)).

### The Core Five Most Influential Books

- **Tao Te Ching** by Lao Tzu — Jack has called this his most prized possession and one of the most influential books in his life ([Business Insider](https://businessinsider.com), [ETCIO.com](https://etcio.com), [Thread Reader App](https://threadreaderapp.com)).
- **The Score Takes Care of Itself** by Bill Walsh (with Steve Jamison & Craig Walsh) — A leadership manual he regularly recommends to entrepreneurs; it embodies culture, discipline, and attention to detail ([Inc.com](https://inc.com)).
- **Between the World and Me** by Ta-Nehisi Coates — Highly regarded by Jack for its socio-historical and reflective narrative ([Inc.com](https://inc.com)).
- **The Checklist Manifesto** by Atul Gawande — He notes this often disappears from his desk and gives copies to employees at Twitter and Square ([Business Insider](https://businessinsider.com)).
- **The Yoga Sutras of Patañjali** (edited by Sri Swami Satchidananda or Edwin Bryant) — A guide to yoga philosophy he keeps among his favorites and features in Square’s library ([Inc.com](https://inc.com)).

### Additional Selections Frequently Mentioned

- *The Four Agreements* by Don Miguel Ruiz  
- *Becoming Steve Jobs* by Brent Schlender & Rick Tetzeli  
- *Civilized to Death* by Christopher Ryan  
- *Whole Earth Discipline* by Stewart Brand  
- *The Plant Paradox* by Steven R. Gundry  
- *Seeing Is Forgetting the Name of the Thing One Sees* by Lawrence Weschler  
- *The Paleo Solution* by Robb Wolf  
- *Principles* by Ray Dalio  
- *The Art Spirit* by Robert Henri  
- *Competing Against Luck* by Clayton Christensen  
- *The Master and Margarita* by Mikhail Bulgakov  

### Particularly, Jack Said

> “The Art Spirit… helped me through many things”  
> “Square would not exist without the work … persistence of Steve Jobs”  
> “Incredible… clarifying.” on *Civilized to Death*  
> “Traveling detail and personal narratives like Clayton Christensen’s perspective…” on innovation books ([Thread Reader App](https://threadreaderapp.com), [Thread Reader App](https://threadreaderapp.com), [Reddit](https://reddit.com))
