# creatine

Creatine is one of the most well-researched performance supplements. For someone sitting around 105–110 lb and looking to build toward a stronger, more athletic body, it is potentially useful as a multiplier—not the primary driver of weight gain.

The primary drivers remain:
- Enough calories
- Enough protein
- Progressive resistance training
- Consistent sleep
- Time

Creatine helps make the training stimulus more productive.

## What 3 Months Could Look Like

Starting point:
- ~105–110 lb

After ~3 months of consistent creatine + adequate food + resistance training:

**Potential range:** ~109–117 lb

This is a rough range, not a prediction.

Some of the initial gain will be:
- Increased water stored within muscle
- Increased muscle fullness
- Additional lean tissue from improved training capacity

Creatine itself does not directly create several pounds of muscle.

A reasonable way to think about the possible progression:

- ~2–4 lb: creatine-associated water / lean mass
- ~1–4+ lb: potential muscle gain from training + nutrition
- Potential total scale weight: ~109–117 lb

The exact result depends heavily on calorie intake, training quality, genetics, and starting muscle mass.

## Why Creatine Helps Muscle

Creatine increases the body's available phosphocreatine, which helps regenerate ATP during short, intense efforts.

This can translate into:

- More reps
- More training volume
- Better repeated high-intensity efforts
- Greater strength development
- Better power output
- Potentially better long-term muscle growth

The important distinction:

**Creatine doesn't replace training.**

It potentially allows you to get more out of the training you're already doing.

## Athletic Benefits

Creatine is particularly relevant for activities involving repeated explosive efforts.

Potential benefits:
- Sprinting
- Jumping
- Short bursts
- Strength training
- Repeated high-intensity efforts
- Faster recovery between intense sets

For tennis, the benefit isn't simply "getting stronger."

It could support the repeated explosive demands of:
- First-step acceleration
- Short sprints
- Direction changes
- Repeated points
- High-intensity rallies

## Mental / Cognitive Benefits

Creatine is not only stored in muscle.

The brain also uses creatine because maintaining cognitive activity requires substantial ATP production.

Research suggests creatine supplementation may provide some cognitive benefits, particularly around:

- Memory
- Mental fatigue
- Cognitive performance under stress
- Processing speed
- Sleep deprivation / fatigue resilience

However, the evidence is considerably less definitive than the evidence for physical performance.

The right expectation is:

**Potentially better cognitive energy/resilience, not suddenly becoming smarter.**

The effect may be more noticeable when the brain is under stress, fatigue, or sleep deprivation.

## Possible Mental Experience

If creatine benefits cognition personally, it could feel less like:

> "I'm significantly smarter."

and more like:

> "I feel a little less mentally drained and can stay engaged longer."

Potentially:
- Slightly better mental endurance
- Less cognitive fatigue
- Better ability to maintain concentration
- Possibly improved memory

But individual response varies, and cognitive benefits are not guaranteed.

## Gut Microbiome

This is an interesting but much less established area.

There is emerging research investigating relationships between creatine and:

- Gut microbiota
- Intestinal barrier function
- Inflammation
- Immune signaling
- Gut physiology

However:

**Creatine should NOT currently be considered a gut-health supplement.**

Evidence for improving the human microbiome is still early compared with the very strong evidence for muscle and performance.

Possible GI effects are more established:
- Some people experience stomach discomfort
- Large doses are more likely to cause GI issues
- Smaller daily doses tend to be easier to tolerate

## Recommended Approach

For this situation, there is probably no need for a loading phase.

**Creatine monohydrate: 3–5 g/day**

Take it:
- Every day
- At whatever time is easiest to remember
- With or without food

Consistency matters more than timing.

Creatine monohydrate is the standard form with the strongest evidence.

## The Bigger Experiment

If the goal is moving from ~105–110 lb toward ~115–120 lb, creatine should be viewed as one component of a larger system.

### Foundation

**Calories**
- Consistent calorie surplus
- Rough starting point: +250–400 calories/day

**Protein**
- Adequate daily protein
- Spread across meals rather than relying on one large meal

**Training**
- Progressive resistance training
- Track lifts/reps
- Gradually increase training stimulus

**Creatine**
- 3–5 g/day

**Recovery**
- Consistent sleep
- Adequate hydration

## Important Insight

If weight has remained around 105–110 lb for a long time, the limiting factor may not be supplementation.

It may simply be:

**Not consistently eating enough calories to move the scale.**

Creatine can help maximize the training response, but it cannot compensate for insufficient energy intake.

The better experiment is:

> Creatine + calorie surplus + protein + progressive training + 12 weeks of consistency.

That gives a much better chance of moving body composition than creatine alone.

## Expected Hierarchy of Benefits

### Strong evidence

1. Increased muscle creatine stores
2. Improved strength / power
3. Better high-intensity exercise performance
4. Increased lean mass when combined with resistance training
5. Increased muscle water / fullness

### Promising but less certain

6. Memory
7. Cognitive performance
8. Mental fatigue resilience
9. Performance during sleep deprivation / stress

### Early / emerging

10. Gut microbiome
11. Intestinal barrier effects
12. Broader metabolic / inflammatory effects

## Bottom Line

Creatine is probably worth considering as a foundational supplement because it has an unusually strong evidence base and relatively low complexity.

For a ~105–110 lb person trying to build a stronger, more athletic body:

**3–5 g creatine monohydrate daily**

combined with:

**eat more → train progressively → recover → repeat**

is the core equation.

The most noticeable early change may be a small increase in scale weight and muscle fullness.

The more meaningful long-term change would be increased training capacity leading to **more muscle, strength, and athletic performance** over months.
